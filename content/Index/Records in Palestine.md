It is possible to sort Palestinian records (as in records by Palestinian musicians) into 3 batches. The first, pre-1948, is a collection of shellac records put out mainly by Lebanese label Baidaphon in the 1920s. Issa Boulos notes a Baidaphon catalog from 1926 that lists at least eight or nine records each for Rajab al-Akḥal, Thurayya Qaddūra, and Nimir Nāṣir, making up a collection of almost 30 shellacs for Palestinian singers before 1926. These catalogs, as I understand, are property of EMI. The second batch (post-48) is now on vinyl and on all kinds of international record labels. Palestinian musicians, at the time composing classical and oriental music (mainly for the PBS), had to flee their homes due to the 1948 [[Nakba]] and were forced to continue their musicianship scattered and in exile where they had their work pressed. The third batch of vinyl was initiated by Fateh in the 60s as part of their rallying. After being gifted a radio station by Jamal Abdelnasser in 1968, Fateh started producing and pressing military Abdelnasser-style marches that marked the era. Palestinian patriotic music has since then begun to take shape and more records followed the official Fateh ones by artists that would flee, for the second time, the Israeli intimidation in 1967. Most of these musical initiatives took place in the diaspora, but music was still happening in Palestine although barely documented. The arrival of the cassette in the late 70s would, however, allow music recording to become more accessible in occupied territories. The only (?) vinyl record I could find of the time by Palestinian musicians based & pressed in Palestine is of the band The Silverstones, which was, alongside others in the “West Bank” inspired by rock music. This record was pressed by an ‘Israeli’ manufacturer in Holon for the lack of Palestinian record labels in Palestine.
