The usage of vinyl for records allowed [[Oil|oil]] companies to put out their own promotional albums pressed on vinyl and Esso (Exxon) went as far as sponsoring steel bands like the [Esso Trinidad Steel Band](https://www.discogs.com/artist/1360987-The-Esso-Trinidad-Steel-Band) and  [Esso Barbados Steel Band](https://www.discogs.com/artist/104675) who carried the oil company name into their very own band name. 

Right before the [[Oil Embargo (1973)|1973 oil embargo]], Gulf + Western, American conglomerate established in 1958 by Charles Bluhdorn and once owned the [Famous Music Corporation](https://www.discogs.com/label/266222) (the worldwide music publishing division of Paramount Pictures) opened [Paramount records](https://www.cvinyl.com/label.php?s=Paramount) in 1969. That same year, Gulf + Western Announced a oil exploration venture in Indonesia as stated in this [article](https://www.nytimes.com/1969/11/04/archives/indonesia-oil-move-by-gulf-western.html) from the NYT. The new Paramount label reissued pop releases by sister label [Dot Records](https://www.discogs.com/label/36601-Dot-Records?page=1), which had recently become a country label and, in the process, had its entire pop back catalog deleted completely. In 1968, they acquired Stax records. They also distributed [Greene Bottle Records](https://www.discogs.com/release/26978030-Spring-Wheel-Always-In-All-Ways-You-Aint-Got-Nothin) (seems to be active only 1972\73). In 1971, [Tumbleweed Records](https://www.discogs.com/label/138177-Tumbleweed-Records-Inc?page=1) was formed by Larry Ray and [Bill Szymczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Szymczyk) with the financial backing of Gulf and Western and was a subsidiary of Famous Music until 1973, when it folded. Gulf + Western also distributed [Just Sunshine records](https://www.discogs.com/label/56719-Just-Sunshine-Records?page=1), record company Active 1971 through 1974, founded by producer [Michael Lang](https://www.discogs.com/artist/283470), co-creator of Woodstock. “For years, he (Lang) was trying to put together big concerts in the Middle East with Israeli and Palestinian musicians performing together at a festival. When you look back after Woodstock, his early career in the ’70s, he was working with artists that he saw beauty and power in their work, perhaps at a time in their career when nobody else saw it. He got a deal with Gulf & Western and started his own label, Just Sunshine Records.” -[Co-Author Of ‘The Road To Woodstock’ Holly George-Warren](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/01/21/qs-with-co-author-of-the-road-to-woodstock-holly-george-warren-on-the-late-michael-lang-and-his-enduring-legacy/)

The merger of oil and music wouldn't stop with the oil shock. In the 1990s, and after Michael Jackson had bought the Beatles catalog, news came out of a joint venture between MJ and alWaleed bin talal. In 1996, Billionaire Prince al-Waleed bin Talal, in a statement sent to Reuters from his head office in the Saudi capital, Riyadh, announced a multimillion dollar show-business joint venture with pop star Michael Jackson and said the business would be called Kingdom Entertainment. One of their first joint activities would be sponsoring Jackson’s world “History Tour,” the statement said. Kingdom Entertainment planned to pursue investments and development projects in a wide range of entertainment activities including animation, theme parks, theme restaurants, character licensing, television programming, motion pictures and multimedia including having Produced the movie Michael Jackson's Ghosts, a 1996 short film starring [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson), directed by Stan Winston, and written by Stephen King and Mick Garris. It is based on a story by Garris, Jackson and King.

AL-Waleed bin Talal is also the owner of Rotana Music group. In 2003, Al Waleed increased his stake to 100 percent, after initially acquiring a 48 percent stake in 2002. Rotana Music Group is the largest record label and music repertoire holder in the Arab world, the company is headquartered in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, with branches in Jeddah, Dubai, Kuwait, Cairo, and Beirut. 
  
This wasn't MJ only ventures in the Arabian gulf. Come 2005 he was living in Bahrain to escape lawsuits against him and he would establish Two Seas Music, a joint venture of Michael Jackson and Sheikh Abdullah of Bahrain. The record label however, didn't seem to release anything.

In October 1979, EMI (Electric and Musical Industries and dubbed Every Mistake Imaginable by John Lennon according to Morrissey) merged with Thorn to form Thorn EMI. [EMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMI) accepted a £169 million offer from [Thorn Electrical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorn_Electrical_Industries) to merge the groups as Thorn saw EMI as a good fit for the future home video market with Thorn manufacturing hardware and EMI providing software. EMI chief executive [Bernard Delfont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Delfont), chairman [Sir John Read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Read) and [Capitol Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records)' chief [Bhaskar Menon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskar_Menon) (son of K.R.K. Menon who was Finance Secretary of India for a short period after Indian Independence from Great Britain from 1949 – 1950.  The first one rupee notes printed by the government of India after Independence bear the signature of KRK Menon) joined the Thorn EMI board, with Read becoming deputy chairman of Thorn EMI. Thorn's chairman Sir Richard Cave became chairman of the merged group.
