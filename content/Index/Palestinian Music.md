The palestinian musicianship in the 70s consisted mainly of an amateur rock band wave that aimed to imitate the global “[[The Beatles|beatlemania]]”. Wither al Baraem (1968-1976), The Red Spots (1974-1977), The Hard Rocks (1972-1976), The Silverstones (1972-1987), or al Kawakib, it seems like these bands all started as “cover bands” before a few of them would go on to make their own music, and I am using the term cover band loosely here because these bands were seemingly more of “imitation bands'' trying to present the closest copy possible of the original. These band members had great relationships with one another and the members would intertwine constantly although an annual music competition would take place at the YMCA in Jerusalem where a lot of these rock bands would go against one another. 

It is not definite how this wave started in Palestine, but it is speculated that the Beatles refueling stop on their way to Hong Kong at Beirut's airport in 1964, might’ve been one reason for the general ripple happening in the Levant, as Lebanon hosted the band, The Sea-Ders, and in Jordan, Believers. In Palestine, Armenian band The Flintstones (1964-1968) was seemingly the first to play cover songs of The Beatles and The shadows (pre-beatle era) in Jerusalem, initiating a wave that later spread across multiple Palestinian cities. Emile Ashrawi from al Baraem remembers a show of The Flintstones at Alhambra cinema in Jerusalem in 1965 to have inspired him largely to start his own band with his siblings and friends.  

Mostly, these bands were self-taught highschoolers who would rehearse and perform at their schools. Hagop Kaplinian from the Flintstones explains ordering an electric guitar from Germany that would get detained in Al Aqaba port (Jordan) for almost 9 months before their school teacher at Frere School went to retrieve it explaining it was a musical instrument for school and comparing it to the Oud. The Silverstones did also rehearse at their school (Frere school) at some point and it is where a lot of the performances took place. 

Not much of this era is recorded or released although just a few years ago (2021) al Baraem released a 4-track EP of original music recorded in Texas after Samir Ashrawi left Palestine in 1973. Al Baraem, which started its journey as a band under the name The blooms (al Baraem in english) had to go through a detour after black september in 1970 according to Emile, while Samir believes it was the [[Naksa|Six-Day War of 1967]] that inspired the band to change their name into arabic and create Arabic songs. In [another article](https://www.al-ayyam.ps/ar_page.php?id=1579191cy360257820Y1579191c), Emile notes 1973 to be the year the switch to Arabic happens as a direct inspiration from the rahbani-fairouz songs that combine both western and eastern influences. 

Around the same time, Palestinian Patriotic music was taking shape and al Baraem for example started collaborating with Mustafa Alkurd who began making patriotic music after the tragedy of 1967. Alkurd's work falls distant from the national liberation songs at the time, mainly produced by Fatah members in exile. His songs were set in a distinct Jerusalemite colloquial dialect and were clearly defined by his personal experiences directly under Israeli occupation. His impact and popularity were far-reaching, eliciting the attention of the Israeli military, which arrested him on several occasions throughout the 1970s and essentially placed him under administrative detention and subsequently forcibly deported him in 1976 which allowed him to press his records in Europe. 

Al Baraem was also one of George Kirmiz earliest music explorations. George kirmiz, who would later become a memorable voice of the first intifada, had started his musical journey with albarem before going solo and releasing two cassettes (one -From Ansar To Askhlan (1985) put out in Michigan). Not much is known about Kirmiz besides his early beginning with al Baraem as he prefers to remain anonymous but he is an important figure in palestinian patriotic music till the day.
