Oil is a [[Monoculture Commodities |monoculture commodity]]. It is [[Neoliberalism|neoliberalism's]] adopted child, and [[Neoconservatism|neoconservatism's]] godchild. The narrative of oil that we are familiar with begins in the 19th century, but it is not until the 20th century where petroleum transforms the globe. Today oil is the highest selling and traded commodity. Oil is intertwined with global warming, politics, national strategies, and battlefields. The history of oil and its uses predates the term history itself. There is evidence from 5000 years ago of oil being used along the Euphrates as a commodity for building mortar. There are descriptions dating back from the first century as oil being used for its medicinal properties. In North America, rock oil was used medicinally by the Seneca (Onöndowa'ga:'). Along Oil Creek in the mid-19th century oil is given a different life; a few investors (James Townsend and George Bissell) and a chemist (Benjamin Silliman Jr.) viewed "rock oil" (petroleum) as a new competitor against the coal industry, specifically in regards to the illumination of lamps through kerosene. By the time they went searching for petroleum the illuminating industry had a source for kerosene through coal and whale fat. What they had intended was for the petroleum to undercut the existing lamp-oil process in the making of kerosene. What was needed was a cheaper extraction of petroleum so that it can be sold at a lower cost than the current lamp oil, and for this they implemented an old Chinese salt boring method within their process. Within a few years of success in boring oil, John D. Rockefeller and Maurice B. Clark would invest in Pennsylvania oil. In 1865, a few months before the end of the Civil War, Rockefeller would buy his partner out of their company for 75,000 dollars. By 1868, Rockefeller was directly invested in the largest oil refinery in the world, and by 1870 the Standard Oil Company was established. A year after Rockefeller bought out Clark, the [[Suez Canal]] was opened, and the same year that Standard was established the [[Telecommunications|telegraph]] had connected England directly to Bombay.
